Example 01

A weekly ops brief, before anyone asks.

Professional services firm
Weekly ops briefChief of Staff agent

Worth a look:

  • Wins: 10 were logged in the last 7 days, but 9 are placeholder opportunities created as already-won on the same day. So the dashboard's "most recent wins" and "days since last close" are misleading. Probably a data-entry pattern to clean up in GHL.
  • Xxxxx Xxxxxx Xxxxx (submitted) has a blocked "RFE Response" task, and is also the oldest unanswered GHL thread, at 195 days.
  • Filed this week: Xxxx Xxxxx and Xxxx Xxxxx. One client's Slack is flagged: the attorney has a question that could put their adjustment of status at risk.
  • Xxxx Xxxxx is a new active matter. Xxxx Xxxxx's list was recreated under a new ID, and the old duplicate lists are gone.
  • Pipeline: 208 open, 64% stuck in Contact Attempted. Calls dropped 38% week over week (66 vs 107), and 40 leads were reached. The old 2026 pipeline still has 418 opportunities left to migrate, almost flat.
  • Ops Review: all 16 "this week priority" tasks are stale. Five haven't been touched in 7 days, including the change-of-attorney letter decision. There are 322 open tasks, 40 completed this week and 33 new. No week-ago snapshot exists yet, so the % change is blank.

Wins, pipeline, client threads and tasks sat in separate tools, so issues only surfaced once someone noticed.

ii.What I built

iii.The result

Clear on what to prioritise this week, with issues flagged before they become a problem for the business.

Example 03

Any question, answered from the source.

Professional services firm
Um, wait, I wanted you to check SOPs in the shared drive. If there is an existing SOP for RFE.
Used 2 tools ›

Yes, there is one, and it's fairly comprehensive. I should have checked Drive first, sorry about that.

"SOP: Request for Evidence (RFE) / Notice of Intent to Deny (NOID) Handling & Pricing Calculation"

It covers:

  • Purpose and scope: RFE, NOID and equivalent notices; applies to O-1, O-2 and EB petitions
  • Roles: admin staff (intake, date-stamping, deadline tracking), case manager plus attorney approval (substantive review, response strategy), finance and billing (pricing, invoicing)
  • Procedure: receipt and intake → initial review and triage (standard vs. complex RFE vs. NOID) → response strategy
  • A library of example RFE responses on file (Xx Xxxxxx, Xxxxx Xxxxx, Xxxx Xxxxxx, Xxxxxxx Xxxxxxx)
  • Pricing framework: priced per page of the USCIS-issued notice (not counting exhibits or attorney drafts), with page count verified by admin and the case handler before billing, plus discount and adjustment rules

So the gap isn't "no SOP". It's that this one exists but clearly isn't being followed consistently, since Xxx Xxxxx's and Xxxx Xxxx's RFEs both had confusion and delays recently. The doc itself looks solid.

Given that, I'd scrap the draft I gave you and instead suggest: don't write a new SOP, audit whether the existing one is actually being used (deadline logged in ClickUp per step 5.1? page-count-based invoice issued per 5.5?). Want me to check either case against these specific steps to see where it broke down?

Answers were buried in shared drives, docs and threads, so finding out something simple meant searching for it yourself.

The answer in one reply, with the source attached and a suggested next step.
